What the activity teaches

Why do the choices matter?

  1. Voltage: A typical red LED needs about 2 V in this simplified model. At 1.5 V, it won't light normally.
  2. Resistance: A resistor reduces the current to help protect the LED. Too little resistance can allow damaging current.
  3. Polarity: An LED must face the correct way to conduct normally.
  4. Closed circuit: An open switch interrupts the path, so current stops.

Our simple equation: current ≈ (battery voltage − 2.0 V) ÷ resistance. When used, this estimates current in amps; the display converts it to milliamps (mA).

Educational model: ideal battery, fixed 2.0 V red LED forward drop, typical 20 mA design ceiling. Actual LEDs vary, and the appropriate resistor must be chosen from the specific LED datasheet. Overcurrent and reverse-voltage choices are deliberately marked as unsafe.
